Cost Platform

The story of replacing SAP Fiori with a custom ReactJS platform for 20,000+ bank employees, told step by step, from the original problem to my role at each stage.

  1. 01
    2018-2020

    The Legacy Problem

    The bank layered SAP Fiori (a role-based tile launchpad) on top of the existing SAP GUI backend to modernize a decades-old transaction system. In practice it introduced new problems: the interface was slow and froze under load, it was hard to customize, and because the tile set was generated straight from a user's SAP role, people ended up with dozens of irrelevant tiles and no easy way to find what they actually needed.

    On top of that, every incremental feature required specialist SAP consultants and a real budget. Small changes were slow and expensive.

    Before: SAP Fiori

    After: Cost Platform (ReactJS)

  2. 02
    My role: UX Researcher

    Data-Driven UX Research

    Every six months I ran a company-wide Voice-of-the-Customer survey to track satisfaction across internal tools, from performance to interface clarity. 1.5 years after the Fiori rollout, that same recurring process is what flagged it as the problem: scores were low across almost every dimension we measured.

    • Exported SAP usage logs and kept only active users, filtered by hours logged and actions taken
    • Segmented that group by role, then by module
    • Invited each segment to a survey and grouped responses by department, region, role and function
    • Scored results with a Top-2 / Bottom-2 box method to surface the sharpest pain points
    • Published a report with scores on one tab and verbatim user pain points on the second

    The output fed a rolling roadmap: priorities for the next six months, then the following year, repeated every cycle.

  3. 03

    The Decision: A Custom ReactJS Platform

    Rather than keep patching Fiori, leadership decided to replace it with a custom shell built on the ReactJS stack. This was my first large-scale project: a small cross-functional team was staffed: one React engineer, a backend engineer, several analysts, a product owner, and me as the designer.

  4. 04
    My role: Product Designer

    Information Architecture

    My job was to make navigation effortless and the platform easy to extend. The requirement on paper was Web-only, but I designed the structure to survive a future move to mobile: Department → Module → links to the underlying systems, grouped by function, everything anchored to a fixed center column to cut cognitive load and keep focus on one task at a time.

    Before this platform, next to Fiori there were more than a dozen narrow, purpose-built internal tools. The plan was to turn this into a single marketplace-style entry point for every financial department: new products built natively in this shell, older tools migrated in over time. The home screen stayed to the essentials: financial news, a calendar, notifications.

    • Hard constraint: the platform could not resemble the bank's two other major internal systems (HR and Service Requests). It needed its own visual identity
  5. 05

    Wireframing & Prototyping

    I prototyped the new structure in Figma, drawing on patterns that already worked elsewhere in the bank: clean layouts from HR-style systems, category navigation from support portals, and card views familiar to Fiori users.

    I tested the light-theme prototype with a control group of users and used their feedback to tighten information density and spacing for smaller desktop screens.

  6. 06

    Design System & Grids

    As the sole designer on the project, I built a design system (grid, components, color tokens) so the development team could ship new services without waiting on design each time.

    Accessibility was a priority: I derived three adaptive shades from the brand color to keep contrast within WCAG guidelines, and we used CSS linting to keep the implementation consistent with the design files. Six months after kickoff, the first live products launched on the platform.

    Grid Variations

  7. 07

    Scaling to Mobile

    The desktop web release shipped in 2021, since the core services lived on the internal domain, but the block-based layout was built to extend without a redesign. In the stages that followed, more departments and services were onboarded, several of the old narrow tools were retired in favor of the platform, and native mobile apps followed the same information architecture in both Light and Dark themes.

  8. 08

    Outcome & Impact

    The platform launched to production within six months of kickoff. The next Voice-of-the-Customer cycle after launch showed satisfaction climbing well past the original baseline, alongside strong adoption across the bank.
